You decide what your space offers its members: Memories, Depot and Expenses, public links, links through which guests
add photos or files, drop boxes, and groups. Whoever runs the server decides first; you can switch off more, never less.

1. Open the menu at the bottom of the sidebar, choose **Settings**, then **Capabilities** under **Shared space**.
2. Each entry says what it is for and whether it is **On**, **Off**, **Off through** the entry above it, or **Off for
   the whole server**. The last one is the server's decision, and only the person who runs the server can change it.
3. Choose **Switch off**. Before anything happens, you read what switching it off does, to what is new and to what
   already exists, and you confirm.
4. **Switch on** brings it back as it was. Switching off deletes nothing at once; only an application left off is
   erased, on the day its row shows.

What switching off does, in short:

- **Public links**, and the two kinds below them, stop at once, including links that already exist: a guest sees the
  same "This link isn't available" page as for an expired link, and nobody can make or renew one. The links work again
  once you switch back on.
- **Groups**: nobody can create a new group. Groups that exist stay usable.
- **Memories**, **Depot** or **Expenses**: nothing new can be added to it, and nothing in it changed — no upload,
  entry, rename, move, cover or new link, and guests can't add anything through a link either. Everything in it still
  opens and downloads, through its links too, and members can still delete things to the trash and restore them.
- **Left off, an application is erased on a day.** Its row shows **Erased on** that day, and every member sees a
  banner naming it, so they have time to download what they want to keep. If the server sends mail, every member is
  also emailed when you switch it off and again a week before the day. On the day, everything in it is erased in your
  space, members' personal spaces included; folders stay. Switch it on before the day and nothing is erased. Switch it
  on later and the erasure still finishes: only what members add after that as something new, such as a new Memory,
  file or Pot, stays. How long the window is, the person who runs the server decides; if they switched the application
  off for the whole server, only they can switch it on in time.

Remembered devices, unlocking without a password and adding a device from another one are offered on the sign-in
screen, before your space is known there, so only the person who runs the server switches them.

## Requirements

Instead of switching something off, you can tighten it. Under **Public links** and **Groups**, each requirement has
**Require** and **Clear**; a number of days is typed first. Before it takes effect, you read what it does to what
exists, and you confirm.

- **Every link needs a password.** Links that already exist without one stop working at once, until their owner gives
  them one.
- **Links expire after this many days.** Links without an end date, or with a later one counted from when they were made,
  stop working at once; a younger link gets an end date that fits, an older one is renewed.
- **Being added to a group needs a yes.** Everyone is asked first, whatever their own setting says. Members already in
  a group stay.

The person who runs the server can set the same requirements. The stricter value is the one in effect: you can tighten
theirs, never loosen it, and the panel says when theirs wins. Clearing a requirement brings every link it stopped back.

Your members see it in their list of links: a link that guests can't open says so, and why (switched off on the server
or in your space, a password missing, an end date too far out). The link's owner fixes a missing password or end date
right there, and the link keeps its address; only a link older than your limit needs **Renew link**, which makes a new
address to hand out. A switched-off kind of link comes back only when you, or whoever runs the server, switch it on.
