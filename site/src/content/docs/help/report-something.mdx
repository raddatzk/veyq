If you see something in a space or through a link that is illegal or against the terms, you can tell the people who
run the server. Only its moderators can read your report. Nobody else learns of it — not the person who shared it, not
the owner of the space.

## Report

1. Choose **Report**:
   - on a folder, a Memory or a file: in its menu, or in its header when it is open;
   - on several at once: choose **Select**, tick them, and choose **Report** in the bar. One report holds up to 100;
     for more, the dialog offers to report the folder that holds them instead;
   - on a photo or video: in the lightbox, the flag button;
   - on a link that only lets you upload: **Report this link** at the bottom of its page.

   If you report one folder, Memory or file, the dialog asks **What do you report?**: **It and everything in it**, or
   **Only its name** — for a name that is the problem while what it holds is not. The moderators then can open its
   name and nothing else: not what it holds, not its other details. At the top of a link you were sent, the name you
   see is the link's: report the folder or Memory itself.
2. Choose **What is it?**: child sexual abuse material, terrorist content, a threat to someone's life or safety, other
   illegal content, or a breach of the terms. For child sexual abuse material you do not need to give your name.
3. Say **Why are you reporting it?** and tick that what you say is accurate and complete to the best of your knowledge.
4. If you want a receipt now and the decision later, give **Your email address**. It is optional.
5. Choose **Send report**. The dialog shows a reference and the time. Keep them if you want to refer to your report.

If **Report** is not there, the server has no moderation key yet, and there is nobody to report to.

## What happens then

- **Only the moderators can read it.** Your device seals your text, your address and the keys to exactly what you
  reported, so that only the moderators of this server can open them. The server sees which items were reported, when,
  and in which category — nothing you wrote. Nothing else of the space opens with those keys; for a name on its own,
  nothing but that name.
- **Nothing is blocked by a report.** A person decides. Until then the item stays where it was, for everyone who had it.
- **What you reported is kept for the moderators.** If it is deleted or changed after your report, the moderators still
  see it as it was when you reported it — for up to 90 days, while they decide.
- **The moderators are told** that there is a new case and its category, never what it is or where. Urgent categories
  reach them at once.
- **Your address** goes to the server once, for the receipt it mails you; it keeps only the copy the moderators alone
  can read. When the moderators decide, they send it once more, and you are mailed whether they took a measure or
  none — nothing about what it was. Six months later that copy is deleted; the server keeps only that you were told.
- **What you can do after the decision** is in the same mail: report it again if you have more information, write to
  the people who run the server at the address they publish (or a note that they published none), go to court, and —
  where the Digital Services Act applies to the server — complain to the Digital Services Coordinator of the country
  you live in (in Germany the Bundesnetzagentur).

## Blocked by the operator

When the moderators decide that something must not be shown any more, they block it. In its place you then see
**Blocked by the operator**, with its name: in the list of a folder, as a tile in a Memory, and in the lightbox. A
member of the space and a guest of a link see the same; only the people it affects are also told why (below).

- **It does not open.** Nothing of it is shown or downloaded, and nothing inside a blocked folder or Memory either.
- **It can still be deleted.** Whoever could delete it before — the person who uploaded it, for example — still can.
  Nothing else reaches it: it cannot be opened, renamed or moved, a file's content cannot be replaced, and once deleted
  it cannot be restored from the trash while the block stands.
- **Nothing is deleted by a block.** When the moderators lift it, it is back as it was.
- **A blocked link** no longer opens for anyone it was sent to: they see it as a link that does not exist. The person
  who made it can neither re-activate nor renew it while the block stands. If the moderators blocked a guest who
  uploaded through a link, that link stops working too, and the person who made it may renew it: the renewed link has a
  new address.

## If something of yours was blocked

If you made the link, uploaded what was blocked, or own the space it lies in, the moderators tell you why.

1. Open what was blocked — the photo in its lightbox, the folder or Memory, or the link in **Share**. Under **Blocked by
   the operator** you find **Why it was blocked**: what was blocked and what that means, why you are told, what the
   moderators found, the ground and the law or the clause it relies on, that a report led to it, that a person decided, that the block applies wherever the server is reached until it is lifted, and that you can go
   to court. If the server sends mail, you also get one saying that this is there — never what the moderators found.
2. If you disagree, write it under **Your objection** and choose **Send the objection**. You can object once. Only the
   moderators can read it.
3. Another moderator than the one who blocked it looks at it again, where the server has one; if not, the same one
   does, and the answer says so. You read the answer in the same place and are mailed that it is there. If the measure
   stands, you can still go to court.

A guest who uploaded through a link is told nothing in veyq of a moderators' block; ask the people who run the server,
at the address they publish. If an authority ordered it removed, the guest's own browser is told on the link
([What a guest sees](/veyq/sharing/what-guests-see/)).

If the people who run the server block your sign-in, or take a space you own offline, because of what was in it, they
mail you their reasons — what they found, the ground and the law or the clause it relies on — since you cannot sign in
to read them. If you disagree, write to the address the mail names.
