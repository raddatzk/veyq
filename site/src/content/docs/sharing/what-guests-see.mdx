A guest opens the link in any browser. Before anything is shown, there may be up to three steps:

1. **This share is password-protected**: they enter the password and choose **Unlock**.
2. **Please accept the terms of use**, if your space has [terms](/space-owners/terms/): they read your terms and,
   after them, the section of the person who runs the server, and choose **Accept and continue**. They are asked once,
   and again when either changes.
   Their acceptance is kept only in their own browser and never sent to the server, so opening the link stays
   anonymous.
3. **Please read this note**, if the note must be accepted: they choose **Accept and continue**. Their acceptance stays
   on their own device; nobody gets a list of who accepted.

<Screenshot id="sharing-guest-view" shows="A shared Memory as a guest sees it, with the badge Shared with you · read-only above the gallery" />

Then they see what you shared, in the same view you have, with a line saying what they may do, for example **Shared
with you · read-only**. The role only takes buttons away; there is no separate guest design.

- With a **view only** link they look around and download: a file as itself, a folder or a Memory as a ZIP.
- With a link that also lets them add things, they are asked **What is your name?** the first time they add something. Afterwards
  the page says **You are here as …**, and **Not you?** lets someone else start over under a new name.
- An upload link or a drop box shows only the page to add things.

A guest who can add things reads, before the first thing they add, that their IP address, source port and the time are kept for a number of days, longer only if a
report or an authority's order concerns what they added, and that they can object. **Who keeps it, why, and your
rights** beside it opens that part of the instance's privacy notice in a tab of its own. Once they have added
something, the line **You are here as …** also shows **Your guest id, to name if you object**, with **Copy**: a guest
has no account, so this id is how the person who runs the server finds what was kept about them. A guest who only
looks is told nothing, because nothing about them is kept. The record is the server operator's, not yours: you cannot
see it.

At the bottom, **How safe is this?** explains, in plain words, why the server cannot read what was shared. Where the
person who runs the server has published who they are, **Legal notice** beside it shows their name, address and email,
and the address that answers users and authorities with its languages (the sign-in page shows it too). **Terms of
this instance** opens the section of the person who runs the server, and **Privacy** their privacy notice, both
readable without an account. Guests never
see who else is in your space, who was there in a Memory, or anyone's picture.

If the link was switched off, has expired or is incomplete, the page says **This link isn't available**.

If an authority ordered something a guest uploaded through the link removed, that guest's own browser — the one they
named themselves in — is told so on the link, even though it answers everyone else as unavailable: that it was removed
on an order of an authority received on that date, and where to ask for the reasons or a copy of the order. If the
server's moderators blocked it — or the guest, or the link — that browser reads their reasons in the same place, and
where the blocked item would be: what was blocked and why, without what they found, which only an account's key opens,
and the address to ask for it and to write to if the guest disagrees. A page that only takes uploads shows every such
reason, whether or not the link still works. Nobody else sees that, and nothing is shown while
an authority has asked the server to wait.

Everything a guest sees is unlocked in their own browser with the key from the link:
[Is my data safe?](/privacy/is-my-data-safe/#when-you-share)
