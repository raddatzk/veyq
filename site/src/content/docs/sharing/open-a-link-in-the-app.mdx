Someone sent you a veyq link, from your server or another one. You can open it in any browser, or in the veyq
[app](/getting-started/install-the-app/). The app needs no account for it: you open the link as its guest, exactly as
the browser would.

## Open it

1. On the app's sign-in screen, or on the screen where you unlock it, choose **Open a link**.
2. Choose **Paste**, or paste the link into the field yourself. The app reads what you copied only when you choose
   Paste, never on its own. Paste the whole link: the part after `#k=` is its key.
3. Choose **Open a link**.

From a share page in your browser, **Copy link and open the app** copies the link and opens this screen in the app,
also while you are signed in: choose **Paste** there ([What a guest sees](/sharing/what-guests-see/#open-in-the-app)).

On a phone you can also **share** the link to veyq: in the chat or the browser where you got it, choose Share and then
veyq. The app finds the link in what was shared, even in a message with more text around it, and opens it as its
guest. If the message holds no veyq link, or more than one, the app says so; then copy just the one link and paste it.
On an iPhone or iPad the link opens in the sheet veyq shows over the app you shared from, and the sheet closes when you
leave the link.

## What you see

Before anything is shown, the link asks what it asks of anyone who opens it: its password, the rules of the space that
shared it, and a note the person who shared it wants you to read. Then you see what was shared, on the same screens the
people of that space use, with only the buttons the link allows: [What a guest sees](/sharing/what-guests-see/).

- With a link that lets you add things, the app asks **What is your name?** the first time you add something.
  **Not you?** lets someone else start over under a new name.
- Before the first thing you add, the app tells you what the server keeps about it: your IP address, source port, the
  time and where it came from, for a number of days. **Who keeps it, why, and your rights** opens the server's privacy
  notice.
- An upload link or a drop box shows only the page to add things.

**Back** inside the link takes you up a level. At the top, it asks **Leave this link?** first: the app does not keep the
link, so to open it again you need it once more. **Leave** takes you back to where you were.

## Keep it

When you are signed in, **Keep** at the top keeps the link as a **bookmark**. Choose where: your own space is offered
first, so the link stays yours alone; in a space you share with others, keeping it there is like forwarding it to them.
Opened from the sign-in screen you are not signed in yet: sign in, choose **New** and then **New bookmark**, and paste
the link there ([Keep a link someone sent you](/sharing/keep-a-link/)).

A bookmark opens in the app as it does in the browser: its page shows the link's content right there, or, where showing
links in place is switched off, opens the link on its own. **Open on its own page**, **Report shared content** and
**Report name** work as on the web.

## Links the app does not open

A moderator's review link, a link of a server that runs another version of veyq, or one of a server that does not
show its links: the app says why and offers **Open in the browser**, which hands the link to your browser, as tapping it
in a chat would. If the link was switched off or has expired, the app says **This link isn't available**.

## What stays private

The link's key stays in the app's memory while the link is open, and nowhere else: not on your device, not in a
request to any server. Where you added something, the app remembers the name you gave in your system's password storage,
so it still knows you next time, until you choose **Not you?**. The server that stores the content sees the same as when
a browser opens the link: [Is my data safe?](/privacy/is-my-data-safe/#when-you-share)
