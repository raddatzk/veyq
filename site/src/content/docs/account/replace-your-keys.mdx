This is for an emergency only: when someone else has your keys, for example because you approved a code that was not
your own new device. The page explains the same before it asks for anything, and none of it is small print.

## What gets replaced

The keys that actually open your data: your **Master Key** and your **identity**, the key everyone else recognises
you by. Your password and your Secret Key stay the same, and your Emergency Kit keeps working.

## Why this step exists

Signing a device out and removing its saved key take away its access to the server, but not the keys it already
holds. With them, that person can keep reading as soon as they reach your encrypted data some other way, and can pose
as you. Only new keys change that.

## Do you need this?

Yes, if a device that is not yours had your keys. No, if you only lost a device of your own: choose **Sign out and
remove this device's saved key** for it instead ([Devices and sessions](/veyq/account/devices-and-sessions/)).

## What changes afterwards

- **To everyone else you are a new identity.** They all see that your key changed, and anyone who had verified you
  has to compare the safety number again.
- **In every shared space you are outside until someone there confirms your new key**, best by comparing the safety
  number over a call: [Verify a person](/veyq/account/verify-a-person/).
- **Every other device is signed out and loses its saved key.** Each one needs your password and your Secret Key to
  get back in, or you add it again from this device. This device also asks for both the next time you sign in here.
- **Group invitations to you that are still open lapse** and have to be sent again.
- **It can't be undone, and it takes nothing back:** what that person already saw stays with them.

Two things before you start:

- **If you are the only owner of a shared space, make someone else an owner first.** Otherwise nobody there can fit
  the new lock, and your old key keeps reading there.
- **You need your Emergency Kit.** That is on purpose: it is the one thing the other person does not have, and it is
  what stops them from doing this to you first.

## Replace them

1. Open the menu at the bottom of the sidebar and choose **Settings**, then **Security**.
2. Under **Replace your keys**, enter **Your password** and **Your Secret Key**.
3. Choose **Replace my keys** and confirm with **Replace my keys** in the dialog.

<Screenshot id="account-replace-keys" shows="The Replace your keys block under Security with what gets replaced, why, when and what changes afterwards, the password and Secret Key fields and the Replace my keys button" />

The page then says **Your keys are replaced** and lists each space with its state:

- **Secured** — this space has a new lock and only your new keys open it.
- **Not secured** — it still has its old lock. Replace your keys again.

Under **Shared spaces** you see the spaces where someone else has to fit the new lock or let you back in:
**Waiting for someone else** means another owner does it the next time they open veyq; **Nobody can do it** means no
other owner is left — make someone else an owner of that space. Until the new lock is in, the old key can still read
what is new there.

If you own a shared space or a group and another member replaced their keys, one owner's device, perhaps yours, fits
the new lock by itself. If that cannot happen, the space shows **Needs a new lock** in the sidebar, and its member
list says why. Either no owner is left who can fit it, so make someone else an owner. Or fitting it kept failing: then
choose **Start again**, and your device takes it on.

Your Emergency Kit keeps working afterwards: your Secret Key stays the same, only what it opens changes.

To the other members, your new keys look like a stranger claiming to be you, which is exactly what veyq is meant to
notice. That is why they confirm it, and why the server alone can never hand your spaces to someone else:
[Is my data safe?](/veyq/privacy/is-my-data-safe/)
