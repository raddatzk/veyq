If someone else got into your account, for example because you approved a code that was not your own new device,
signing their device out is not enough: it already holds your keys. Replacing your keys shuts it out from now on.

Read these four points first. The page shows them too, and none of them is small print:

- **It stops what comes next; it can't take back what's gone.** Anything that person already saw, they still have.
- **Every other device is signed out and loses its saved key.** Each one needs your password and your Secret Key to
  get back in, or you add it again from this device. This device also asks for both the next time you sign in here.
- **You need your Emergency Kit.** That is on purpose: it is the one thing the other person does not have, and it is
  what stops them from doing this to you first.
- **In shared spaces, someone else has to confirm it is really you** before you are back in, best by comparing the
  safety number over a call: [Verify a person](/veyq/account/verify-a-person/). In a space only you own, make someone
  else an owner first, or nobody there can let you back in.

## Replace them

1. Open the menu at the bottom of the sidebar and choose **Settings**, then **Security**.
2. Under **Replace your keys**, enter **Your password** and **Your Secret Key**.
3. Choose **Replace my keys** and confirm with **Replace my keys** in the dialog.

<Screenshot id="account-replace-keys" shows="The Replace your keys block under Security with its four points, the password and Secret Key fields and the Replace my keys button" />

The page then says **Your keys are replaced** and lists each space with its state:

- **Secured** — this space has a new lock and only your new keys open it.
- **Not secured** — it still has its old lock. Replace your keys again.

Under **Shared spaces** you see the spaces where someone else has to fit the new lock or let you back in:
**Waiting for someone else** means another owner does it the next time they open veyq; **Nobody can do it** means no
other owner is left — make someone else an owner of that space. Until the new lock is in, the old key can still read
what is new there.

Your Emergency Kit keeps working afterwards: your Secret Key stays the same, only what it opens changes.

To the other members, your new keys look like a stranger claiming to be you, which is exactly what veyq is meant to
notice. That is why they confirm it, and why the server alone can never hand your spaces to someone else:
[Is my data safe?](/veyq/privacy/is-my-data-safe/)
