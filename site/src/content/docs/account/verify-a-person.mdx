Every member has a **safety number**, made from their key. When yours and someone else's app show the same number
for that person, you know the server gave you their real key and not someone else's. Comparing it once is optional;
it matters most when a key has changed.

## Compare a safety number

Do this together with the other person, side by side or on a call.

1. Open the other person's profile: choose their name where veyq shows it, for example among who was there in a
   Memory, or as who added a file or photo.
2. Under **Safety number**, choose one:
   - **Scan their code**, and point your camera at the code on their own profile page.
   - **Compare digits**, read the digits aloud with them, and choose **They match** or **They don't match**.
3. If they match, the page says the person **is verified on this account**, and shows **Verified**.

The other person finds their own number on their own profile page, under **Your safety number**. They open it the
same way, by choosing their own name, for example on a file or photo they added.

<Screenshot id="account-safety-number" shows="Another member's profile with the Safety number block: the badge Not verified, the digits, and the Scan their code and Compare digits buttons" />

If the numbers do not match, do not mark the person as verified: the key you have may not be theirs. Ask them to
check their own profile, and compare again.

## When a key has changed

If the server starts showing a different key for a member, veyq notices: their profile and the member lists under
**Settings** show **Key changed** and a **Review key** button. This happens when someone
[replaced their keys](/veyq/account/replace-your-keys/), and it is exactly what you would also see if someone tried
to pass off a key as theirs.

1. Choose **Review key**. The dialog shows two numbers: **Expected — what you trusted** and **Current — shown now**.
2. Ask the person directly, outside veyq, whether they replaced their keys, and compare the current number with the
   one on their own profile.
3. Only when you are sure it is really theirs, choose **Accept new key**. Otherwise choose **Refuse**.

<Screenshot id="account-review-key" shows="The Review changed key dialog with the Expected and Current numbers and the Refuse and Accept new key buttons" />

Until you accept, your device hands that member no space key and does not add them to a group: a changed key could
be a new device, or someone pretending to be them. Accepting does not verify the new key; compare again afterwards
if you want it verified.

veyq only ever trusts a key your own device has checked; the server can pass keys along, but it cannot swap one in
without your app showing it: [Is my data safe?](/veyq/privacy/is-my-data-safe/)
