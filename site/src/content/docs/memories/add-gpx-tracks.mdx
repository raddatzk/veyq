A Memory can show the way you went: a hike, a ride or a sailing day, recorded by your watch, your phone or a bike computer
as a **GPX file**. Each track is drawn as a line on the Memory's map, beside the photos you took along the way. A Memory
and each of its chapters can hold their own tracks.

## Add a track

1. Open the Memory (or the chapter) and choose the **Map** tab.
2. Choose **Add GPX track** and pick one or more `.gpx` files.
3. Give each track a **name** and an **activity** if you like, then choose **Add track**. Both are optional: without a
   name, a track is named after the name inside the file, or else after the file. **Cancel** adds nothing.
4. Each track is listed under the map with its colour, its name and how far, how long, how far up and how far down it
   went, and drawn on the map with a green ring where it starts and a red one where it ends.

A track is named after the name inside the file, or else after the file, until you give it a name of your own. veyq checks each file on your device before it
uploads it, and tells you which one it did not add and why: a file larger than 25 MB, a file that is no GPX track, a track
with fewer than two points, or a Memory that already holds 50 tracks.

## On the map

The map shows your photos where they were taken (each photo at its own location) and every track you have not hidden.
Untick a track to hide it from the map; click a line or a track's name to pick it out, and the map shows that track
whole.

The map needs **Map services**, the same setting every map in veyq uses ([Set the location](/memories/set-the-location/#map-services)).
While they are off, the tab offers to turn them on; the list, the numbers, the download and the removal work without
them. A track's line never leaves your device: the map provider only learns which part of the map you look at.

## Name a track and give it an activity

You can name a track as you add it, or later: the **⋯** beside a track offers **Edit**: give the track a **name** and an **activity** - hiking, walking, running,
cycling, mountain biking, road or gravel cycling, skiing, water or other. The activity is shown above the track's name and
drawn as the line's pattern: a solid line on foot, a dotted one running, a dashed one cycling, and so on, so you can tell a
walk from a ride on the map. Empty the name to name the track after its file again. Everyone who may change a photo's date
may edit a track; people you share a link with see the name and the activity, but cannot change them.

## Download or remove a track

The **⋯** beside a track offers **Download GPX**, which saves the file as it was added, and **Remove**. A removed track
goes to the Memory's **Trash** tab with the photos you removed, and you can bring it back from there
([Remove photos and bring them back](/memories/photo-trash/)). **Report** is there too, as for a photo.

Tracks are not photos: they are not in the gallery and not in its count, but a download or a copy of the Memory carries
their files, with the name and the activity you gave them.

## Who can do what

Everyone who may add photos to the Memory can add a track, and everyone who may remove photos can remove one. People you
share a link with see the tracks as you do; with a link that lets them add photos, they can add a track, with a name
and an activity, and remove their own.

The server stores each track encrypted, like a photo, and cannot even tell that it is a track: it never sees where you
went.
