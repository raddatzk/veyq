1. Open the folder and click the file's row. Its own page opens with the file on it.
2. Look at it, as described below.
3. To keep a copy on your computer, choose **Download**. It is also the download button on the file's row. You get the
   file exactly as you put it in, under the name it has in veyq.

<Screenshot id="depot-file-page" shows="The page of an opened PDF with its header (name, size, type, modified, Uploaded by) and the first page of the PDF below" />

## What is shown

- A **picture** is shown.
- A **text** (plain text, Markdown, code, JSON, CSV and the like) is shown as text. Select it as you like, or choose
  **Copy**. **Wrap lines** switches line wrapping on and off. A long text shows only its first megabyte.
- A **PDF** is shown page by page, with its page count and buttons to **Zoom in**, **Zoom out** and **Fit to width**. You can
  select and copy its text. Links inside a PDF do nothing, so a PDF can never take you somewhere else.
- A **video** or a **sound file** plays. Use **Full screen** for a video.

A **PDF that is protected by a password** asks for it right on the page. The password is used only on your device to
open the PDF; it is not stored or sent anywhere. If you cancel, the download is offered instead.

Some things cannot be shown on the page: a format your browser cannot display, and very large pictures or PDFs
(over 64 MB). Whenever something cannot be shown, veyq says so and the **Download** button is right there.

The file is unlocked on your device while you look and nothing extra is stored. Someone you shared the file with sees
exactly the same.

## Who uploaded it

At the top of a file's page, members of the space see **Uploaded by**: the member's name and picture, **Former member**
for someone who has left the space, or the name a guest gave when the file came in through a link. After a
[replace](/depot/replace-a-file/) it names whoever replaced it. People you share a link with never see this.

## Very large files

Large files are downloaded piece by piece and put back together on your device. Browsers differ in how well they cope.
Where your browser can save straight to a file you choose, that works best; where it cannot, a very large file may be
refused with a message instead of failing halfway.
