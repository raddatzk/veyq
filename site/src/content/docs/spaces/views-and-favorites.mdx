## Folders and contents

A folder shows everything in it in two groups: first its sub-folders, then its **contents** — Memories, files and drop
boxes together, in alphabetical order, whatever each is; numbers count as numbers, so "Photo 2" comes before "Photo 10".
Where a folder holds both, the two groups are headed **Folders** and **Contents**.

Each entry says what it is: for example "File · 157 B · modified …" or "Memory · June 2024". The most useful button (for a
file, download) sits on the entry; the rest are in its **⋯** menu. Where you may not do something, its button is not
there.

## Tabs

When a folder holds more than one kind of thing, tabs above the list — **All**, **Memories**, **Depot**, **Expenses** —
show just one application's things; drop boxes are under **Depot**, with the files. Sub-folders stay visible in every
tab. The tabs share their row with **Select**, the **Trash** and the view buttons, at its right end; **New** sits
beside the folder's name.

## Grid or list

Use **Grid view** and **List view** at the right end of the row above the list, and **Map view** wherever the tab
shows Memories. This browser remembers your choice.

In the list, each entry is one line with a small picture in front. In the grid, the folders are small tiles; everything
else is a card: its picture on top, its name below it inside the card. The heart and the **⋯** of a card appear on its
picture when you point at it or move to it with the keyboard; on a phone they are always there.

## Favorites

Every entry has a **heart** (**Favorite**). It adds the entry to **Favorites** in the sidebar, which lists the
favorites of the space you are in and appears once you have one; **Remove favorite** takes it out again. If a favorite
cannot be saved, the heart goes back and a message says so. Your favorites are yours alone: other members of the space cannot see them, and
neither can the server. People you share a link with get no heart.

## Addresses you can bookmark

The address of a folder in your browser reads like its path, for example `…/#holidays/2026/`. A folder's address ends
with a slash. A Memory sits under `/memories/`, for example `…/#holidays/memories/rome`, and a chapter follows its
Memory: `…/#holidays/memories/rome/first-day`. In your personal space the address starts with `~` and your username, for
example `…/#~anna/taxes/`; in a group it starts with `@` and the group's name, for example `…/#@family/summer/`. If two
of your groups have the same name, the address carries a code instead of the name. You can bookmark any of them.
The readable part comes after the `#`, which a browser never sends to a server, so the names in the address stay on your
device.
