# Inbox

The Sammelbecken: every topic and idea that still needs handling. Low friction — drop a row,
refine later. Status flow: `inbox → refining → refined → planned → in-progress → done`.
When a topic is refined, its ideas move into durable state under [`features/`](features/),
[`crosscutting/`](crosscutting/), [`decisions/`](decisions/) (+ `contracts/`) and the row's status advances.

> **A done row is deleted** (owner 2026-10-19), like a CR when it is done: what it taught lives in the ADRs,
> features and coverage it changed, and its history in git (`git log -S'| <n> |' -- docs/inbox.md`). A row that is
> built but still owes a measurement or a proof is not done. **Row numbers stay reserved and are never reused** — a
> citation `#<n>` in a doc, a test or a commit keeps naming that one topic. **Highest number used so far: 599** —
> the next row is 600; raise this line with every new row.
>
> An open row holds its title, tags, status, the next step and the links that matter — no history. Its status names
> where it stands against V1: **V1** (on the V1 line), **end of V1** (needs a remote or a release, done at the very
> end of V1), **after the V1 queue** (the owner's own passes — real devices and browsers, the CAX measurement — once
> the V1 queue is through) or **out of V1** (§ V1 — the line, *Out of V1*).

> **A row at `inbox` has no prior work behind it — only a title.** These rows were once pointers into a
> previous implementation that has since been deleted; what they pointed at is gone. Several are large
> (the product vision, goals & stakeholders, legal, search, journal). Refining one means **deciding it
> fresh, with the user** — not recovering it.

> Single-file by choice. It is already large; once it needs scrolling to navigate, split by area (one file
> per area) rather than let it rot.

## V1 — the line

Planning lives here by convention ([docs/README](README.md) § use-case), so the release scope does too. The
*system's* goals and quality bars are not this — they are [architecture](architecture/README.md) §1 and §10,
and this section is downstream of them.

**V1 is self-hosted.** No public instance operated by us. The audience is **one person who hosts for themselves
and their circle** — technical enough for `docker compose` **or for `helm install` on a cluster they already
run** (decided 2026-09-09: Kubernetes **gates** V1, it is not post-V1 scope —
[ADR 0064](decisions/0064-kubernetes-helm-deployment-target.md), whose "alongside — not instead of" describes
the two targets and was never a statement about timing) — and the same human as the operator. Share-link
recipients are *guests*, not audience: they must not be overwhelmed, but nothing is built to win them over.

That single decision settles most of the scope below: with no central operator there is nobody to verify email
addresses against and nobody to sign a service promise. It does not remove moderation: whoever hosts for their
circle is still a host for every link their circle shares (**#462**).

**The line widened on 2026-09-20, and what that costs is recorded here rather than discovered later.**
Everything raised that day went in on one owner's call — #136–#140 and the seven open CRs. Only one of them gates
a release: `cr-change-password-cold-credential-gate`, a live bypass of a decision already taken. The rest is
interface work and code health, added to a line that until then was made of durability and operations debts
(**#26**, **#12**, **#102**, **#89**/**#90**) — and those four now ship *after* it, not before. Two of the
additions carry an open product question rather than a task: **#139**'s four forks (whose privacy default
*"everyone's favorites"* picks, against the enabler's own stated fix) and **#140**'s predicate. Both are decided
in their refine, before anything is built. The trade was named and taken, not overlooked.

**Standing scope rule for V1 (owner's call 2026-10-03):** everything that concerns veyq's **stability, quality or security** goes on the V1 line — bugs, races, guard and test gaps, honest docs, hardening — while new features stay after V1 unless the owner names them. A new finding is classified against this rule when it is filed; ask only where it is genuinely unclear.

**The line widened again on 2026-09-23 — by a whole review.** The security/reuse review of that day filed
#151–#175 and `cr-inert-store-responses`, and all of it went on the line on one owner's call. The rows with a live
exploit path are what the release most needs, and most of them are small: `cr-inert-store-responses` (stored XSS
on the app origin), #152 (first-run setup takeover), #156 (cross-tenant recovery tokens), #157 (pending invitees,
suspension bypass), #153 (unbounded presigns), #175 (targeted login lockout). Five rows are design work decided
in their refine: #152, #154, #157, #159, #160. The largest single item is #161's protocol half (pairing becomes a
PAKE); it was first ranked behind native clients, because against V1's operator-served web client it protects
nothing, and it went on the line knowingly. #151 is cheapest now: before V1 there is no installed store to
migrate. The trade was named and taken, not overlooked.

notifications hub (**#84**), live co-editing (**#6**), on-device agent work (**#10**), SaaS and billing (**#41**).

**End of V1 and after the V1 queue (owner's call 2026-10-19).** What needs a remote or a release — the first
publish (**#26**), the first `backend-races` log (**#317**), the yearly train's release procedure (**#436**), lanes
L2 and L3 and the promotion (**#453**, **#460**, **#461**) and everything the first push proves (**#471**; lane L1's workers per instance, **#548**, only if that run asks for them) — is done
at the very end of V1. The CAX measurement (**#476**) and the real-device and other-browser passes (**#221**,
**#260**, **#268**, and the non-Chromium halves of the rows that say so) are the owner's, after the V1 queue.

Also out, and moved rather than dropped: **email verification (#112)**. It was in V1 only as recovery's second factor, and ADR 0054 shipped that factor as the token mailed at recovery time — address control proven when it is used, not stored from registration. **Back on the pre-V1 list 2026-09-28 (owner's call)**, built 2026-10-01 (`cr-email-address-confirmation`, [ADR 0074](decisions/0074-address-control-is-recorded-where-nothing-else-proves-it.md)): a recorded, non-gating confirmation. *(This paragraph used to say it returns with "#43 open self-registration" — #43 is at-rest PII encryption; no row owns open self-registration.)*

Public share moderation was out of V1 on the argument that with no central operator there is no one to quarantine on
anyone's behalf. **Back on the line 2026-10-16 (owner's call, #462):** the self-hosting operator is that one.
