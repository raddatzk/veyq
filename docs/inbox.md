# Inbox

The Sammelbecken: every topic and idea that still needs handling. Low friction — drop a row,
refine later. Status flow: `inbox → refining → refined → planned → in-progress → done`.
When a topic is refined, its ideas move into durable state under [`features/`](features/),
[`crosscutting/`](crosscutting/), [`decisions/`](decisions/) (+ `contracts/`) and the row's status advances.

> **A row at `inbox` has no prior work behind it — only a title.** These rows were once pointers into a
> previous implementation that has since been deleted; what they pointed at is gone. Several are large
> (the product vision, goals & stakeholders, legal, search, journal). Refining one means **deciding it
> fresh, with the user** — not recovering it.

> Single-file by choice. It is already large; once it needs scrolling to navigate, split by area (one file
> per area) rather than let it rot.

## V1 — the line

Planning lives here by convention ([docs/README](README.md) § use-case), so the release scope does too. The
*system's* goals and quality bars are not this — they are [architecture](architecture/README.md) §1 and §10,
and this section is downstream of them.

**V1 is self-hosted.** No public instance operated by us. The audience is **one person who hosts for themselves
and their circle** — technical enough for `docker compose` **or for `helm install` on a cluster they already
run** (decided 2026-09-09: Kubernetes **gates** V1, it is not post-V1 scope —
[ADR 0064](decisions/0064-kubernetes-helm-deployment-target.md), whose "alongside — not instead of" describes
the two targets and was never a statement about timing) — and the same human as the operator. Share-link
recipients are *guests*, not audience: they must not be overwhelmed, but nothing is built to win them over.

That single decision settles most of the scope below: with no central operator there is nobody to moderate for,
nobody to verify email addresses against, and nobody to sign a service promise.

Nothing is lost by relaying the key instead: in *that* direction the scan authenticates nothing anyway (the attacker authors the QR, therefore the key), so the out-of-band channel buys no key authenticity — the transcribed code does, and the answer binds them. The second marker is not cosmetic: the payloads are byte-identical, so without it the sign-in card's scan entry would read one as the other, post a bare transport key, and **burn** the write-once answer slot before any screen looked wrong. **(b)** Answered, and it settled the phase order: **no `contracts/crypto` vector, so slice 2 is a pure web slice** — but *conditionally*, on the CR's first decision, that the new device's ephemeral transport key is a full `Identity` bundle. The general claim that a new `asymWrap` context needs a vector is **false in this repo**, measured from two angles: `content-key`, `media-meta`, `node-meta`, `scope-epoch` and `share-key-recovery` are live in `web/src` and appear **0×** in `contracts/crypto/vectors/v1.json` — the vectors accreted per-CR with no stated rule, which is a small durable-state gap of its own. **(c)/(d)** Both decided in the CR: one scanner entry on the sign-in form dispatching on the payload prefix, and the trusted device stops polling the instant it has the answer and never polls again — a collect after `seal` would spend its own grant. **Three findings the refine raised that outlive the CR:** the coverage criterion `the-locked-entry-adds-no-shell-route` is written about the *QR-sign-in* entry, yet slice 2 puts the **first** new option on that same sign-in card, so either the criterion covers both entries or slice 2's has none; and `jsqr@1.4.0` (cozmo/jsQR) becomes load-bearing for a second, security-critical flow while it is **de facto abandoned** (measured 2026-08-13). **Settled 2026-08-13, and it left durable state rather than a note:** jsQR is kept as a deliberate, granted exception to the root `CLAUDE.md` no-abandoned-dependency rule, because every alternative is worse *here* — and the exception, its measurement and the three triggers that reopen it now live in [`web/CLAUDE.md`](../web/CLAUDE.md) § Conventions, which is where someone about to "fix" the stale version will actually read it. `KitScanner.vue` had **no test at all**, so the reuse refactor landed with that component's first harness — `web/src/features/account/emergencyKit/KitScanner.test.ts` exists (measured 2026-08-18). **Slice 2 may land alone on the condition that the `wiki/` section is assembled only after slice 3** — measured, `wiki/` carries nothing on device linking today; An onboarded device would therefore be signed in for **exactly one page load**, holding a `DEVICE_KEY` wrap it can never reach again and no password to fall back to. **That transport correction is BUILT 2026-08-13** (`cr-delegated-session-cookie-transport`, deleted): `POST /api/token/adopt` on the auth spec, a second method on `TokenResource` inheriting its class-level `@PermitAll` + `@RateLimit(IP)`, and `TokenAdoptSliceTest`. The refine that produced it began by re-deriving the finding instead of inheriting it — which is what caught that **one third of it was false**. The two mechanical claims hold verbatim (`boot()` refreshes with an empty body; `TokenResource.kt:36` is `if (fromCookie != null)`). The rejection reason did not: keeping the token in JS was called *"the first multi-tab-unsafe session in the client"*, and it would not have been the first — measured, **nothing** in `web/src` serializes refresh, `boot()` refreshes in every tab, and the loser of a concurrent refresh reads `SUPERSEDED` and revokes the family, so ADR 0004's clone case is reachable **today, with cookies**. That is now **#123**, its own row, because it predates device linking and touches every web session. The cookie survives as the answer for the reason ADR 0004 § Context already gave (a body token is one XSS away) plus one the CR adds: a cookie is self-updating across tabs where a script-held copy goes stale silently. **Four decisions the refine settled, all now in durable state rather than in the CR:** adoption is a **new verb** (`POST /api/token/adopt`, second method on `TokenResource`), because `/api/token/refresh` deliberately cannot change a lifetime and a cookie-less refresh has none to inherit — a `sessionScope` there would let every refresh re-decide its own; it is **not narrowed** to delegated families, because the family row records no origin *by decision* (ADR 0004 § Session family) and the narrower channel-keyed variant is impossible — `collect` marks the channel terminal, so nothing survives to check against; the **lifetime is chosen on the new device**, by the same single "stay signed in" question login asks, which also drives the `DEVICE_KEY` choice (E5, ADR 0005 §22 now carries the password-less carve-out); and the response is **`SessionResponse`, not `TokenPair`**, because of a second inherited-value miss the refine found — write moment 1's device↔family join is keyed by family id, `PairingDelegateResult` carries only a token, and without the id the onboarded device is never *named* in the sessions list, which the use-case's own postcondition demands. **Slice 2 is COMPLETE 2026-08-13.** Its new-device half and the shared tail landed in the order ADR 0059 §2 forces — adopt *before* unlock, register and enrol: `onboardTail.ts` (which slice 3 reuses rather than rebuilds), the routeless join card the sign-in form's ONE scan entry dispatches to on the payload prefix, a sibling grant poll whose stop condition is the inverse of the trusted device's, and a Master-Key unlock export so `openVault` stays the single choke point into the vault. One attempt is therefore structural, the transcription is editable until it is sent, and the new device learns a refusal only as the channel going quiet, which its copy says. The **deliberate slide moved to LAST**, after the proof holds: that is what the built *add a device* direction already does, a gesture spent before the evidence exists cannot be about the evidence, and taken last the refusal costs the user nothing — with the slide first, a wrong code spends the whole ceremony and hands back nothing, which reads as *"it glitched, try again"*, the exact reflex ADR 0059 §7 is written against. And that leaves **two gates in series** (proof verified AND slide completed), either of which alone satisfies the criterion's *no envelope is ever posted* — so they are tested with one bite each, because a suite driving only a wrong code cannot say which one held. **What remained on this row was the live obligation, not a slice — and it is now discharged:** master-key + identity rotation was refined 2026-08-14 into [ADR 0060](decisions/0060-key-rotation-after-compromise.md) and **built 2026-08-16** as row **125**. A phished grant is therefore *stoppable* — never reversible, and no surface of the flow may imply otherwise. Two directions ship together (*add a device* / *sign in with a QR code*), the grant carries the Master Key **and** a session so no password is typed on the new device, and each direction earns a different binding step — compare a code where both screens are in front of you, transcribe one back where they are not. **The sequencing reason was half wrong and the correction is load-bearing:** #111 does not make a phished grant reversible — it makes it *containable and visible*.
