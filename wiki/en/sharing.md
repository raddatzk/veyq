# Sharing with people who have no account

A plain-language companion to [Is my data safe?](is-my-data-safe.md) and [Files](files.md). You can hand
something to anyone — a grandparent, a friend, a group chat — as a **link**. They open it in a browser: no
account, nothing to install.

## What you can share

- **A folder**, with everything in it: files, Memories, further folders.
- **A single file** — the folder it sits in stays private; nobody can see the rest of it through that link.
- **A Memory** (or one of its chapters).

Choose **Share** on it and create a link. The link shows only what you shared, never the rest of your space, and
you can switch it off any time. You can also put a **password** on it, so a link that lands with the wrong person
still doesn't open.

## What the other person may do

When you create a link you decide **what people may do** — in the dialog, the question is *What can people do?*

| Choice | What it means |
|---|---|
| **View only** | They can look and download. They can change nothing. |
| **View + add** (for a folder: *View + contribute*; for a Memory: *View + add photos*) | They can look **and** add their own things. In a folder they can also move things around inside it and put a newer version over a file. |
| **Add only** (Memories: *Add photos only*) | A **drop box**: they can hand things in but **cannot see** what is there or what anyone else added. |

A person with a "view + add" or drop-box link types their name **once**, so you can see who added what. Other
visitors of the link never see that name.

Two honest details:

- A guest with a "view + add" link on a folder can **replace** any file in it, yours included. veyq warns them first,
  and there is **no version history** — the old content is gone. Guests cannot **delete** files.
- Someone who hands things in through a drop box sees what they just added only in that browser tab. It's for
  *handing things in*, not for managing a gallery. You see everything.

## A drop box for files

A **drop box** (in Files) is a place of its own that collects files from people without an account. Create it with
**New** → **New drop box**, then make a link. Everyone who uses the link gets **a folder of their own** in it, so nobody
sees what others sent — and you and the other members of your space see all of it in one place. Switching the link
off keeps everything that already arrived. If you want to keep a guest's folder, you can move it out as an ordinary
folder; that person can then no longer add anything to it.

## A share looks like your own view

What a guest sees is **the same view you have**, with the same layout, grid and list. The role of the link only
**removes functions**: a "view only" guest simply doesn't get the buttons to add or move. There are no special
guest screens that work differently.

## Switching off, renewing and deleting

- **Switch off** stops the link from working; what guests already added stays with you.
- **Renew** gives a link a new address and deletes the old one — hand out the new one. Do this when someone left
  who might still hold the old link.
- A switched-off link can be **deleted** for good.

## Why this stays private

The secret that opens a link lives **in the link itself**. It never reaches our server, and the files and names are
locked on your device and opened only in the browser of whoever holds the link. So a shared link is readable by
link-holders and still not by the person running the server. Guests' files count against the space you allowed
that link.

The honest limit, same as everywhere: in a browser, this is as private as your trust in whoever *serves* the web
page — if that is you (self-hosted), it is fully private. And a link can't be unseen: revoking it stops future
access but can't erase what someone already saved. More: [Is my data safe?](is-my-data-safe.md#when-you-share).
